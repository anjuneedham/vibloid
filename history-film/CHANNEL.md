# Past Tense: channel identity

NOTE: I could not check name or handle availability on YouTube from here. Search YouTube for the name and your preferred handle before committing, and do a quick trademark search if you plan to build a brand around it.

## Name

**Past Tense** (recommended). A pun: history is in the past tense, and the stories are tense.

Backups if it is taken: **Dust & Daggers**, **The Backstory Bureau**, **Ink Stained History**.
Handle ideas: @PastTenseHistory, @PastTenseToons, @WatchPastTense.

## Tagline

Strange, dark and overlooked history. Told with a straight face and a crooked smile.

## YouTube "About" bio (paste into the channel description)

Past Tense is a cartoon history channel for people who like their facts with a side of "wait, that really happened?"

Every day we dig up a strange, dark or overlooked story from history: forgotten rulers, baffling decisions, mysterious disappearances and the small human details textbooks skip. Everything is hand-drawn in code, written and narrated by a real human, and sourced.

New video every day. If you like history that is smart, a little funny and never boring, subscribe and come back tomorrow for the next one.

Dramatised cartoon retellings. Sources are listed in every video description.

(Short version for the profile line, 160 characters: "Cartoon history for the curious: strange, dark and overlooked stories, told with dry humour and real sources. New video every day.")

## Upload plan

- One 5-minute video every day, same time each day.
- Work in batches: write and record 5 to 7 scripts in one sitting, so voiceovers are not a daily chore. Keep a buffer of finished videos in case of a missed day.
- Reuse the shared characters, props, backgrounds and cards across films so each new video is mostly new story, not new art.
- Add Shorts later by cutting the funniest or most shocking 30 seconds from each film.
- Playlists: Forgotten Rulers, Mysteries, Terrible Decisions, Strange Places, Wars You Skipped.

## First 10 episode ideas (all real, well-documented, safe to dramatise)

1. The man who was declared dead on paper and had to prove he was alive (Lal Bihari and India's "living dead" land-record cases).
2. The Great Emu War (Australia, 1932).
3. The Dancing Plague of 1518 (Strasbourg).
4. The Emperor Norton: the man who declared himself Emperor of the United States.
5. The War of the Bucket (Modena vs Bologna, 1325).
6. The Tulip Mania myth: what really happened.
7. The Cadaver Synod: a pope put a dead pope on trial (897).
8. The London Beer Flood of 1814.
9. The Year Without a Summer (1816) and how it created Frankenstein.
10. The Siege of Paris zoo: when Parisians ate the zoo animals (1870).

Before filming any of these, we write a sources file and double-check the key facts.

## Script template

- 700 to 800 words, short sentences, written to be spoken aloud.
- Numbered beats, 8 to 14 per video.
- Each beat: spoken lines, a one-line visual idea, and the sources for any claim.
- Read it aloud once before recording. Mark pauses with a slash (/).
