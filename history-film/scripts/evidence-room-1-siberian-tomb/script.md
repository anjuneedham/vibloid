## BEAT 1: THE CASE (0:00 to 0:25) | Evidence Room, Curator centre
[CURATOR] Ladies and gentlemen, the court of history is now in session.
[CURATOR] Today's case: a tomb in Siberia, a thousand cannabis seeds, and one very big claim.
- Panels: (A) wide, Curator at the lamp, caption "THE EVIDENCE ROOM". (B) close-up monocle. (C) gavel slam, starburst "ORDER!".

## BEAT 2: THE CLAIM (0:25 to 1:05) | Dig takes the stand
SOURCE TAG on screen: "Science, Nov 2026 (reported by ScienceDaily and AP)"
[DIG] Siberia! A huge burial mound called Tunnug 1, in Tyva, in the south of Siberia!
[DIG] And inside the central chamber, researchers report somewhere between a thousand and fifteen hundred cannabis seeds and fragments!
[CLERK, caption stamp] Source: study published in Science, November 2026.
- Panels: (A) Dig points at a mound drawn in cartoon style. (B) seeds spill across the panel, counter pops. (C) Clerk stamps "SOURCE".

## BEAT 3: THE DATE (1:05 to 1:45) | chalk-style date card inside a panel
[CURATOR] Radiocarbon dating of six of those seeds places them in the early eighth century BCE. That is somewhere around twenty-seven or twenty-eight hundred years ago.
[DIG] The oldest directly dated evidence of cannabis used to get high! That is the claim!
[DOUBT] Hold on. "Used to get high" is doing a lot of work in that sentence.
- Panels: (A) big "8th century BCE" card with timeline strip. (B) Dig bursting with joy. (C) Doubt, one eyebrow raised, bubble.

## BEAT 4: THE HAIR (1:45 to 2:35) | the key evidence
SOURCE TAG on screen: "AP report, Nov 2026: outside researchers"
[CURATOR] The seeds are not the key evidence. The hair is. Researchers report finding traces of THC, and a related compound called CBN, in strands of human hair from the central chamber.
[DIG] Proof they used it!
[DOUBT] Or proof the hair spent two thousand years next to a pile of cannabis.
[CURATOR] That is the objection. One outside researcher said the data do not distinguish consumption from external exposure. Hair buried beside the seeds could simply have picked up residue.
- Panels: (A) hair strand under a magnifying glass. (B) Dig and Doubt face off. (C) Clerk holds up a card "EXTERNAL EXPOSURE?".

## BEAT 5: THE NUMBERS (2:35 to 3:05)
[DOUBT] Another outside expert called the CBN levels "hard to believe" for wild cannabis.
[CLERK, caption] Contested. Both views are in the sources.
- Panels: (A) big "HARD TO BELIEVE" speech bubble. (B) Clerk stamps "CONTESTED" in red.

## BEAT 6: THE GREEK WITNESS (3:05 to 3:55) | Herodotus
SOURCE TAG on screen: "Herodotus, Histories 4.73 to 4.75 (Godley translation)"
[CURATOR] Why does this matter? Because the ancient Greek writer Herodotus described Scythian people using hemp seeds. He places it after a funeral, as a way to cleanse themselves.
[DIG] He wrote that they crawled under felt covers, threw hemp seeds on red-hot stones, and howled with joy in the vapour!
[DOUBT] Herodotus wrote that centuries after this tomb. And historians debate how much of it he saw himself.
[CURATOR] Archaeologists place Tunnug 1 in the oldest Scythian horizon. But the tomb does not establish whether cannabis was consumed during the burial ceremony itself.
- Panels: (A) cartoon scroll "HERODOTUS" with felt tent drawn in flat shapes. (B) steam burst. (C) Doubt shrugs.

## BEAT 7: THE EVIDENCE BOARD (3:55 to 4:25) | Evidence Room pin-board
[CLERK] Seeds: found, and radiocarbon dated. Hair chemistry: contested. Use in burial ceremonies: not established.
- Panel: one wide panel, the pin-board with three cards pinned and red string, each stamped (green FOUND, red CONTESTED, grey NOT ESTABLISHED).

## BEAT 8: THE VERDICT (4:25 to 4:50) | slow push-in
[CURATOR] The court finds: the seeds are real. The dates are solid. The high is unproven.
[CURATOR] Verdict: plausible, not proven.
[DIG] Plausible is still exciting!
[DOUBT] Plausible is where arguments start.
- Panels: (A) Curator with gavel. (B) giant stamp "PLAUSIBLE, NOT PROVEN" with starburst, flash, page shake.
