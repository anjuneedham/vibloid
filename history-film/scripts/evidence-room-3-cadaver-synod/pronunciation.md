# Pronunciation guide (Part 3, The Cadaver Synod)

Checked 2026-11-20. VERIFIED means a dictionary or reference gives it (source named). UNVERIFIED means no dictionary or institution source was found: treat as a guess, and ask a native speaker or listen to an audio clip from a reliable source before recording. No audio could be played in this session, so every entry rests on written transcriptions (IPA or respellings). Never present a guess as verified.

Wikipedia and Wiktionary are crowd-edited: they count as "reference-level" only. Dictionary.com and Merriam-Webster count as dictionaries. Merriam-Webster's own lowercase "lateran" entry (a Scottish variant of "lectern") is NOT the Roman Lateran: do not use it.

Same pronunciation every time in the film. Where a name is UNVERIFIED, the script can often avoid saying it (see "Safe workaround").

## Formosus
- Say: **for-MOH-sus** (stress on MOH).
- Latin pronunciations (Wiktionary, entry "formosus"): Classical [foːrˈmoː.sʊs]; Italianate Ecclesiastical [forˈmɔː.s̬us] (their symbols). https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/formosus . The Latin word means "good-looking, well-formed".
- English clue: Merriam-Webster gives the English place name Formosa as fȯr-ˈmō-sə, which supports "for-MOH-". https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/Formosa
- Status: Latin IPA VERIFIED (Wiktionary). The English respelling for-MOH-sus is OUR reading of that IPA and of Formosa: UNVERIFIED as a dictionary entry for the pope's name. No English dictionary lists "Formosus" itself; Wikipedia's page for the pope gives no pronunciation.
- Fun fact (attribute): the name means "handsome".

## Stephen VI (also called Stephen VII)
- Say: **STEE-vun**. VERIFIED: Wiktionary /ˈstiːvən/ ("very rarely /ˈstɛfən/"). https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/Stephen
- Say the number in words: **"Stephen the Sixth"**. Some older lists call him "the Seventh". Why: the Stephen elected on 23 March 752 died three days later, before consecration; older lists counted him as Stephen II, and the Vatican's Annuario Pontificio dropped him in 1961, which moved every later Stephen down one number (Wikipedia, Pope-elect Stephen). Britannica uses "Stephen VI (or VII)", the New Catholic Encyclopedia "Stephen VI (VII)", the 1913 Catholic Encyclopedia "Stephen VI, not Stephen VII". Pick VI and show "(some lists: VII)" once on screen. Status: numbering VERIFIED; pronunciation VERIFIED.

## Arnulf (of Carinthia)
- **Arnulf**: German IPA /ˈaʁnʊlf/ (Wiktionary: https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/Arnulf ). No English dictionary pronunciation found. English respelling **AR-nulf** (rhymes with "car" + "gulf" roughly) is OUR reading: UNVERIFIED.
- **Carinthia**: kuh-RIN-thee-uh. VERIFIED: Merriam-Webster kə-ˈrin(t)-thē-ə (stress on RIN). https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/Carinthia
- Recommended: "AR-nulf of kuh-RIN-thee-uh". Safe workaround: "the German king Arnulf" with the spelling on screen.

## Spoleto
- Say: **spoh-LAY-toh**. VERIFIED: Merriam-Webster spō-ˈlā-(ˌ)tō (alternate ending -ˈlē-). https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/Spoleto
- Wikipedia (lead): /spəˈleɪtoʊ/ default; US /spoʊˈleɪtoʊ, spoʊˈliːtoʊ/; UK /spoʊˈlɛtoʊ/; Italian [spoˈleːto]. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spoleto . (Wikipedia's Italian transcription is as shown; an Italian speaker may say spo-LEH-to.)
- Status: VERIFIED (dictionary + Wikipedia). Recommended: spoh-LAY-toh.
- "Duchy of Spoleto" / "the Spoletans": say "the Spoleto family" or "the family from Spoleto" to avoid the adjective (the adjective "Spoletan" is UNVERIFIED).

## Ageltrude (also spelled Agiltrude)
- No dictionary, institution or reference pronunciation found. Wikipedia (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ageltrude) gives none. **UNVERIFIED.**
- Our guess, clearly a guess: **AJ-el-trood** (Wikipedia notes the variant spelling Agiltrude). Do not present it as correct.
- Safe workaround: say "Lambert's mother, the empress" and show the name in text only. Or ask a medievalist or a Lombard-Italian name expert for a recording. (Reminder from sources.md: Ageltrude is Lambert's MOTHER, Guy's wife.)
- Also in the cast: **Lambert** (LAM-bert, ordinary English name) and **Guy** (GEE or GY, English: "Guy"; Italian forms "Guido"/"Wido" appear in sources). The Italian "Guido" (GWEE-doh) is UNVERIFIED here. Say "Guy".

## Lateran
- Say: **LAT-er-un** (stress on LAT).
- VERIFIED: Dictionary.com, American [lat-er-uhn] /ˈlæt ər ən/; British /ˈlætərən/ (used for "Lateran Council" and "the Lateran"). https://www.dictionary.com/browse/lateran
- Status: VERIFIED.
- Note: no source we read actually names the Lateran as the venue of the synod (see sources.md). If the script says "the Lateran", frame it as "traditionally".

## Liutprand (of Cremona)
- No IPA or respelling found. Wikipedia lists the spellings Liutprand, Liudprand, Liuprand (and Luitprand in some books). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liutprand_of_Cremona . **UNVERIFIED** for every pronunciation.
- Plain guess often heard in English: **LYOOT-prand**. Do not present as verified. Other readings exist ("LEE-oot-prand").
- **Cremona**: kreh-MOH-nah. VERIFIED: Merriam-Webster krā-ˈmō-nä. https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/Cremona
- Safe workaround: say "Liutprand, an Italian bishop who wrote about 60 years later", with the spelling on screen, and use the same reading every time. Or search for an audio clip from a university medieval-studies department.
- Book title: **Antapodosis** ("Tit-for-Tat" in F. A. Wright's translation; English title "The Book of Retribution"): UNVERIFIED pronunciation. Say "his book Tit-for-Tat" or just "his chronicle".

## Tiber
- Say: **TY-ber** (rhymes with "fiber").
- VERIFIED: Merriam-Webster ˈtī-bər (https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/Tiber); Wiktionary UK /ˈtaɪbə/, US /ˈtaɪbəɹ/ (https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/Tiber).

## Synodus Horrenda
- The Latin phrase "horrenda synodus" ("the horrible synod") is in the 898 council acts as quoted by Dümmler (see sources.md). "Synodus Horrenda" as a modern label: the Latin pronunciation of the whole phrase is **UNVERIFIED**.
- Word clues: Wiktionary gives Latin synodus Classical [ˈsy.nɔ.dʊs], Ecclesiastical [ˈsiː.no.dus] (https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/synodus). Wiktionary has no entry result for "horrenda" in that fetch. A common English reading is "SIN-oh-dus ho-REN-da": OUR guess, UNVERIFIED.
- Safe workaround: say **"the Horrible Synod"** (English translation of the Latin), show "Synodus Horrenda" in text, and do not read the Latin aloud. Or have a Latinist record the Latin once.
- Note: "synodus" gets two valid Latin readings (classical SOO-no-dus vs church SEE-no-dus); no single "correct" one.

## Cadaver Synod
- **Cadaver**: kuh-DAV-er. VERIFIED: Merriam-Webster kə-ˈda-vər ("a dead body"). https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/cadaver
- **Synod**: SIN-ud. VERIFIED: Merriam-Webster ˈsi-nəd, also ˈsi-ˌnäd. https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/synod
- Status: VERIFIED. Say "kuh-DAV-er SIN-ud".

## Porto
- The bishopric of Formosus (Latin Portus, the harbour of ancient Rome at the Tiber mouth; Wikipedia, Diocese of Porto-Santa Rufina).
- Italian: **PORT-oh** (stress on the first syllable, open "o"). Wiktionary Italian "porto" (harbour/port): /ˈpɔr.to/. https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/porto . Status: IPA VERIFIED (Wiktionary) for the Italian common noun "porto"; the name of the see is the same word, so the reading follows. UNVERIFIED as a dedicated entry for the diocese.
- Warning: do NOT say it like the Portuguese city "Porto" (Wiktionary: Portugal /ˈp(w)oɾ.tu/). Different place, different sound. Our film's Porto is Roman. Say "the bishop of Porto, the old harbour of Rome".
- Safe workaround: use the English "Porto" once with the explanation "the old Roman harbour town" and do not stress the vowel.
