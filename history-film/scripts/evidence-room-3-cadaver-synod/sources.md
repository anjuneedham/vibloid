# Sources: Part 3 (The Cadaver Synod, Rome, 897)

Checked 2026-11-20 by web search, page fetches and full-text reading of three Internet Archive / PDF scans (Liutprand in English, Dümmler's Latin edition of Auxilius and Vulgarius with his German commentary, and Gregorovius in English). Britannica pages returned HTTP 403 to the fetch tool, so Britannica lines below come from search-result summaries only (marked REPORTED). The Oxford Dictionary of Popes (J. N. D. Claim on trial (as briefed): "In 897 a pope put his dead predecessor's corpse on trial."

Claim as it can safely be worded (see "Corrections to the brief"): "In early 897, Pope Stephen VI had the dug-up body of Pope Formosus put on trial in Rome."

Status key: VERIFIED = text read by us, or two or more independent reliable sources agree. REPORTED = one reliable source says it, or it came through a search or fetch summary and must be re-read before quoting. MUST VERIFY = check a print or primary source before it goes in the script. NOT SUPPORTED = the evidence does not back it, or points the other way.

Notes on method:
- "Read in full" texts are OCR scans. The meaning is reliable; check exact spelling on the page image before putting any phrase on screen. Page numbers are the printed numbers as seen in the OCR and may be off by one.
- English quotations from the Catholic Encyclopedia, New Catholic Encyclopedia and Wikipedia came back through a fetch tool that summarises pages. Re-read them by eye before on-screen use.
- Latin and German passages translated below are OUR translations. Label them "our translation" or have a Latinist check them. Never put an unchecked translation of ours inside quotation marks on screen.

## F. Delightful but verifiable details for a 5-minute film

1. Formosus means "good-looking" in Latin. VERIFIED (Wiktionary). No pope has used the name since; in 1464 Pietro Barbo wanted to be "Formosus II" and was talked out of it. REPORTED (Wikipedia citing Pastor; Gregorovius).
2. The accuser had committed the same "crime". Stephen had moved from bishop of Anagni to pope, the exact charge he brought against Formosus. VERIFIED (NCE: "his election broke the law against transferring a bishop"). And the earlier pope Marinus I had moved from the see of Caere (Dümmler). VERIFIED.
3. Annulling Formosus's ordinations threatened Stephen's own status if Formosus had made him a bishop. REPORTED (NCE; Dümmler "soll"). Attribute.
4. The corpse had an official "lawyer": a deacon appointed to answer. VERIFIED (annals).
5. The judges had been Formosus's own clergy, and later claimed they were forced. REPORTED (Auxilius; 898 acts).
6. The same Lateran basilica fell in the same year. VERIFIED for the collapse; cause and order DISPUTED. Present it as "the church fell down that year; people read it as a sign" (Gregorovius p. 226: "Men were not wanting who ... saw an omen").
7. The body, thrown in the river, was found again and carried into St Peter's. Fishermen (Liutprand) or a monk (Auxilius). VERIFIED that it was recovered; show both versions.
8. The next popes tried to undo it, and a council ruled that a dead man cannot answer to a court. REPORTED (Gregorovius quoting the Latin canon; MUST VERIFY).
9. The reversal itself reversed: in 904 Sergius III, who had sat in Stephen's synod, undid Formosus's ordinations again. VERIFIED.
10. Pope Stephen's own end: seized by a mob, thrown in prison, strangled. VERIFIED (summer 897).

Three best dramatic beats (all attested; keep gore off-screen):
- BEAT 1, THE COURTROOM: a robed figure in a chair, a lone deacon told to speak for it, the pope asking the question. Source: deacon (annals, CE); question as Liutprand reports it (attributed). Cut away before any mutilation.
- BEAT 2, THE TWIST: the prosecutor is guilty of the same offence (a bishop who moved to Rome), and the church building itself falls down that year. Sources: NCE, Dümmler; Ann. Alamannici and Council of Ravenna c. 10 for the collapse. Stephen is seized and killed in prison within months.
- BEAT 3, THE VERDICT ON THE VERDICT: the body in the Tiber is found and reburied; two later councils condemn the synod and declare that a dead man cannot be tried; then Sergius III reverses it again in 904. Sources: Auxilius, Liutprand, Dümmler, Gregorovius, CE.

## G. Advertiser-friendly handling (mistreatment of a corpse)

How to handle:
- No on-screen mutilation, no decomposing body, no blood, no strangling. Use the Evidence Room devices already in the series: the Clerk's quill, a sealed evidence bag, a cut to black, an empty chair with a folded robe, a cartoon silhouette or a covered figure with its face hidden by the tall mitre. A card that says "Accounts disagree: 2 fingers or 3?" (text only) is better than any picture.
- Attribute every harsh detail: "Liutprand wrote ...", "a defender of Formosus said ...". Do not say them in the narrator's own voice.
- Use verbs that imply: "stripped of his robes", "his blessing hand was mutilated, chroniclers say", "thrown into the Tiber". Do not describe the state of the body.
- Strangulation: "reportedly strangled in prison", one short line, off-screen.
- Keep the tone aimed at power and absurdity (a pope prosecuting a corpse, the bad logic of the charge, the church condemning it a year later). Do not mock the Church or Catholics as a group. The Church's own councils condemned the synod; say so (CLAUDE.md: humour targets power, never religious groups).
- Teaser safe: yes. "A pope puts a dead pope on trial" is accurate and mild. Avoid thumbnails with a decomposed face or a skeleton close-up.

Most disputed, best handled as a contradiction card (not stated as fact): number of fingers (2 vs 3), who recovered the body, whether Stephen or Sergius is Liutprand's pope, earthquake vs age, role of Lambert and Ageltrude, exact date and building.

Most lurid, best left OUT: blood flowing from the corpse's mouth (Laubach, Benedict of Soracte), saints' images bowing (Liutprand, hearsay), the corpse being dragged by the feet or through the streets (Invectiva, Hermann, Gregorovius's colourful retelling), "the head was cut off" (NOT SUPPORTED), "mummy", "half-rotted", poison, Arnulf's "beheading Roman princes" (Liutprand I.28), the mob "yelling", "insane fury" (Gregorovius's own colour, not in any source).

## Contradictions to show on screen

1. WHICH POPE? Liutprand (about 960) says "Sergius" ordered the exhumation (Wright I.30). Sergius III became pope only in 904. Dümmler (1866) says Liutprand's chapter 29 describes Sergius's failed bid of 898 and that Liutprand merged Stephen and Sergius. Wikipedia (Sergius III): Liutprand "apparently misplaced the Cadaver Synod under Sergius". The Clerk: "The best-known storyteller got the pope wrong." Note: Wikipedia's Cadaver Synod page and Medievalists.net quote Liutprand's question as Stephen's; the text says Sergius.
2. TWO FINGERS OR THREE? Liutprand: three. Auxilius (a defender writing about 10 to 15 years later, not an eyewitness): "duobus" (two). The Annals say neither.
3. THE BODY'S CONDITION. The Invectiva calls it "marcidum et paene in puluere redactum" (withered, almost turned to dust, Dümmler p. 10 n. 4); Auxilius says it was whole, by God's grace; Chronicon S. Benedicti praises its incorruption. Show as a text card, no image.
4. BURIED FIRST, OR STRAIGHT INTO THE RIVER? Auxilius: buried among strangers, later dug up and sunk with weights. Annales Laubacenses, Hermann: dragged and thrown in. Fulda: ordered buried outside the proper place.
5. WHO FOUND HIM? Liutprand: fishermen. Auxilius: a monk, after a vision, 20 miles downstream, body still whole. CE: a monk.
6. WHEN? Annals file the trial under 896; Dümmler says about February 897; Gregorovius February or March 897; modern books January 897. Gap after death: 8, 9 or 11 months.
7. WHERE? "In Rome" is certain. "At the Lateran" appears in modern books but no source we read says it.
8. EARTHQUAKE OR OLD AGE? CE, Wikipedia, Britannica: earthquake. The 898 council and annals: "destroyed" / "fell". Dümmler and Gregorovius: age. Order: Annales Alamannici put the collapse BEFORE the trial; Baronius puts it AFTER as a punishment; popular accounts put it during.
9. WHO WAS BEHIND IT? Old view: Lambert and his mother Ageltrude (Duchesne, Dümmler, Gregorovius). Duhr (1932): Lambert approved the annulment at Ravenna, so he is unlikely the architect. Arnaldi (1951): Guy IV of Spoleto. Monroe: no reliable source ties Lambert to it; Stephen acted from weakness.
10. THE OATH. Auxilius and CE: Formosus swore at Troyes 878 never to return to Rome. CE: the acts of that synod are "of doubtful authenticity". Marinus I freed him from it in any case.
11. UNANIMOUS ELECTION? Wikipedia: unanimously elected 891. Liutprand and Gregorovius: a rival faction backed Sergius.
12. AFTERMATH NUMBERS. Wikipedia: John IX "excommunicated seven cardinals". Dümmler: seven bishops and two priests were pardoned. Gregorovius: followers of Sergius excommunicated.
13. WHEN DID STEPHEN DIE? Dümmler and most books: July or August 897. Gregorovius: autumn 897.
14. "STEPHEN VI" OR "VII"? Both used (see section B).
15. THE MISTAKEN OPENING WORD. The brief's phrase "his dead predecessor": Formosus died April 896; Boniface VI reigned 15 days; Stephen followed Boniface. Formosus was an earlier pope, not Stephen's immediate predecessor.

## Corrections to the brief

- Ageltrude was Guy's WIFE and Lambert's MOTHER, not Guy's mother (Wikipedia Ageltrude; CE Kirsch).
- Stephen VI was not Formosus's immediate successor (Boniface VI, 15 days, came between). "His dead predecessor" is loose; "an earlier pope" is safer.
- Liutprand does not blame Stephen; he blames "Sergius" (see contradiction 1).
- The Lateran collapse is real but "earthquake" is later; the oldest texts only say it fell. Do not call it an earthquake in the narration without attribution.
- "Cardinals excommunicated by John IX" (Wikipedia) is not supported by the sources we read.
- The Lateran as the venue is the modern default, not a documented fact.

## Recommended verdict

Claim as reworded ("In early 897, Pope Stephen VI had the dug-up body of Pope Formosus put on trial in Rome"): PROVEN.

Reasons for the Curator: it is described by the German annals of the 890s, by official councils within a year (898, which condemned it), by Auxilius and Vulgarius within about fifteen years, and by Liutprand later. Even Stephen's own pro-Stephen epitaph and Sergius III's later reversal show the synod was real and politically live. The hostile and friendly sources disagree about the details, not about the event.

Split rulings (stamps for the evidence board):
- Corpse exhumed and tried by a synod in Rome under Stephen VI: PROVEN.
- A deacon spoke for it: PROVEN (annals, CE).
- Formosus was convicted and his acts annulled: PROVEN.
- The body was thrown into the Tiber and later recovered and reburied: PROVEN (how it was found: disputed).
- Stephen was imprisoned and killed that summer: PROVEN (killer unknown, "strangled" reported).
- Later popes condemned it (Theodore II, John IX) and Sergius III partly undid that in 904: PROVEN.
- Exact date (January) and building (Lateran): UNPROVEN.
- Three fingers cut off (vs two): PLAUSIBLE; the count is disputed.
- Lambert and Ageltrude ordered it: PLAUSIBLE but disputed; Monroe says no source ties them to it.
- The Lateran collapse was divine punishment or an earthquake: UNPROVEN (collapse itself is PROVEN; the sources do not say earthquake).

The topic is well supported. No alternative from the backup list (Canute, Alfred and the cakes, King John and the Wash) was needed, so none was researched.
