# Pronunciation guide (Part 2, Caerleon)

Checked 2026-11-20. VERIFIED means a dictionary or reference gives it (source named). UNVERIFIED means no dictionary or institution source was found: treat as a guess and ask a native speaker or listen to a Museum Wales / Cadw / BBC Wales audio clip. No audio could be played in this session, so every entry rests on written transcriptions. Never present a guess as verified.

## Caerleon (the English name)
- Say: **kar-LEE-un** (stress on the middle syllable).
- VERIFIED (dictionary transcriptions, two sources that agree on the stress):
  - Dictionary.com: American [kahr-lee-uh-n], /kɑrˈli ən/; British /kɑːˈlɪən/. https://www.dictionary.com/browse/caerleon
  - Wikipedia: /kərˈliːən/, respelled "kər-LEE-ən". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caerleon
- Note: the first syllable is "car" or a schwa, depending on the source. The "ae" is not pronounced as a separate sound in English.
- Recommended: **kar-LEE-un**, same every time.

## Caerllion (the Welsh name)
- Wiktionary gives Welsh IPA: South Wales /kei̯rˈɬiɔn/, North Wales /keɨ̯rˈɬiɔn/. https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/Caerllion
- In plain letters: roughly **kyre-HLEE-on** (a rhyme of "fire" for the first syllable, then the Welsh "ll", then LEE-on). The Welsh "ll" is a breathy "l" made by putting the tongue in the "l" position and blowing air round the sides. There is no English letter for it. A local news piece (inkl, "11 Welsh place names that Newport has made its own") spells the Welsh version "Kyre-lee-on" (informal, not a dictionary). https://www.inkl.com/news/11-welsh-place-names-that-newport-has-made-its-own
- Status: IPA VERIFIED (Wiktionary, a crowd-edited dictionary, so "reference-level" only). The plain-letter respelling above is OUR approximation = UNVERIFIED.
- Recommended: the user may say "Caerllion" once, slowly, on screen with the spelling. The Welsh "ll" is hard for English speakers and a poor attempt on a history channel is risky: either ask a Welsh speaker for a recording, or say "Caerllion, the Welsh name" in the English sound and show the spelling. Do not claim it is correct.
- Meaning: "fort of the legion" is the accepted reading (Wikipedia, citing Hywel Wyn Jones, The Place-Names of Wales, 2005). Wiktionary says the second element is "uncertain, possibly lleng (Roman legion)". "Caer" means "fort" (Wiktionary).

## Isca Augusta
- Latin name of the Roman fortress. No dictionary or institution pronunciation found. **UNVERIFIED.**
- Common English reading (a guess, not checked): **ISS-ka aw-GUS-ta**.
- Safe option: say "Isca" (ISS-ka) and use "the Roman fortress of Isca"; Museum Wales and Cadw themselves use "Isca" alone. The name comes from the River Usk (Museum Wales).

## Legio II Augusta
- Say in English: **"the Second Augustan Legion"**. Museum Wales and Cadw use this form. No pronunciation needed. VERIFIED as the form the museums use: https://museum.wales/blog/1326/Caerleon---City-of-the-Legion/
- If the Latin form is read aloud (LEG-ee-oh, "legio secunda Augusta"): UNVERIFIED. Avoid.

## Geoffrey of Monmouth
- **Geoffrey**: JEFF-ree (ordinary English name). Common word, no source needed.
- **Monmouth**: **MON-muth**. VERIFIED: Wikipedia gives /ˈmɒnməθ/, respelled MON-məth, with a variant /ˈmʌn-/ (MUN-). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monmouth,_Wales
- Recommended: "JEFF-ree of MON-muth".
- **Historia Regum Britanniae**: say its English title, "The History of the Kings of Britain". The Latin reading is UNVERIFIED; do not say it aloud.

## Wace
- Wikipedia gives two English readings: /weɪs/ (rhymes with "place") or /wæz/ ("waz"). In French [vɑs]. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wace
- Status: the two readings are VERIFIED as listed by Wikipedia (not a dictionary). Neither is "the" correct one.
- Recommended: **WAYSS** (rhymes with "place"). Show the spelling once: "Wace of Jersey".
- Roman de Brut: "ro-MAHN duh BROOT" is a plain French approximation = UNVERIFIED. Safer: say "the Roman de Brut, a French poem about Britain" once, or just "Wace's poem".

## Giraldus Cambrensis (Gerald of Wales)
- Dictionary.com gives British IPA /dʒɪˈrældəs kæmˈbrɛnsɪs/. https://www.dictionary.com/browse/Giraldus-Cambrensis
- In plain letters: **jih-RAL-dus kam-BREN-sis**. Status: IPA VERIFIED (one dictionary, British); the respelling is ours.
- Recommended: say **"Gerald of Wales"**, and "Giraldus Cambrensis" once, with the on-screen spelling. Do not attempt the Welsh form (Gerallt Gymro): UNVERIFIED.
- Itinerarium Cambriae: say "The Journey Through Wales" (the usual English title).

## Tennyson
- **TEN-ih-sun**. VERIFIED: Wikipedia gives /ˈtɛnɪsən/. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred,_Lord_Tennyson
- "Alfred, Lord Tennyson". **Idylls of the King**: the pronunciation of "Idylls" (usually EYE-dulz in English) is UNVERIFIED here. Look it up in Merriam-Webster before recording.

## amphitheatre
- **AM-fuh-THEE-uh-ter** (British "amphitheatre", American "amphitheater"). VERIFIED: Merriam-Webster respelling ˈam(p)-fə-ˌthē(-ə)-tər, also ˈam-pə-ˌthē-. https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/amphitheater
- Note: the "(p)" is optional, so "AM-fuh-" and "AM-puh-" are both listed.

## Cadw
- **KAD-oo**. Wikipedia: IPA [ˈkadu]; Wiktionary Welsh pronunciation North Wales /ˈkadʊ/, South Wales /ˈka(ː)du/. https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/cadw
- A children's encyclopedia gives the spelling "CAD-oo" (kiddle.co, low authority). Cadw's own website gives no pronunciation guide. https://cadw.gov.wales/about-us
- Status: IPA VERIFIED (Wiktionary). "KAD-oo" (the "a" as in "cat", "oo" as in "too") is our respelling of that IPA: UNVERIFIED as a respelling, but safe.
- Meaning: "a Welsh word meaning 'to keep' or 'to protect'" (Cadw). Good line for the Clerk: "Cadw, the Welsh word for 'to keep'."

- **Usk** (the river): **USK** (rhymes with "husk"). UNVERIFIED here; widely known. Welsh name Wysg (UNVERIFIED).

- **Mortimer Wheeler** and **Tessa Wheeler** (full name Tessa Verney Wheeler): ordinary English names, no guide needed.
- **Nemesis** (Roman goddess on the curse tablet): NEM-uh-sis, a common English word.
- **Daily Mail**: ordinary.
- **Camelot**: not needed for this episode (Caerleon is "Caerleon").
- **Historia Brittonum** and **Nennius**: UNVERIFIED. Say "an old Welsh history from about the year 830".
