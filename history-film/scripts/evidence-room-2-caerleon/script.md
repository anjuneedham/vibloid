## BEAT 1: THE CASE (0:00 to 0:25) | Evidence Room, Curator at the lamp
Source tag: none (title beat)
L01 [CURATOR] Ladies and gentlemen, / the court of history is back in session.
L02 [CURATOR] [S] Today's case: a Roman ruin in Wales, / and a legend that calls it King Arthur's Round Table.

## BEAT 2: THE CLAIM (0:25 to 1:00) | Dig takes the stand
Source tag: "Museum Wales; Ordnance Survey maps, recorded 1898 to 1908"
L03 [DIG] Caerleon, in south Wales! / A Roman fortress, home of the Second Augustan Legion!
L04 [DIG] [S] And outside the walls, / a huge oval arena. / Old maps label it King Arthur's Round Table!
L05 [CLERK] Source: Museum Wales. / The fortress was founded in A-D seventy-four or seventy-five.

## BEAT 3: THE ROMANS (1:00 to 1:50) | the Romans are not in doubt
Source tag: "Cadw; Museum Wales"
L06 [CURATOR] The Romans are not in doubt. / Cadw says Caerleon was one of just three permanent legionary fortresses in Britain, / built to hold down a fearsome Celtic tribe.
L07 [CURATOR] Museum Wales says it held over five thousand soldiers.
L08 [CURATOR] The amphitheatre was begun around A-D ninety, / with room for about six thousand. / That number is an estimate.
L09 [CURATOR] [S] Cadw calls it / the Roman equivalent of today's multiplex cinema.
L10 [DOUBT] [S] A cinema. / Not a throne room.
L11 [CLERK] Noted: one museum page says A-D eighty.

## BEAT 4: THE WITNESS (1:50 to 2:30) | Geoffrey of Monmouth
Source tag: "Geoffrey of Monmouth, History of the Kings of Britain, Book IX ch. 12 (Giles translation); Cadw"
L12 [CURATOR] The legend starts with a book: / The History of the Kings of Britain, / written about eleven thirty-six by Geoffrey of Monmouth.
L13 [DIG] He says Arthur held a magnificent court / in the City of Legions! / That is Caerleon!
L14 [CLERK] Cadw describes Geoffrey as / "the somewhat imaginative twelfth-century scholar."
L15 [DOUBT] Somewhat.

## BEAT 5: THE MISSING TABLE (2:30 to 3:20) | the twist
Source tag: "Geoffrey, Book IX ch. 13 (Evans translation); Wace, Roman de Brut, 1155"
L16 [CLERK] We searched Geoffrey's book: / two translations and the Latin.
L17 [CLERK] [S] No Round Table. / No amphitheatre.
L18 [CURATOR] His guests sit at a table / "according as the rank of each did demand."
L19 [CURATOR] The Round Table, / where nobody ranks higher, / first appears in a poem by Wace, in eleven fifty-five.
L20 [DIG] So Arthur's table arrives almost twenty years later!
L21 [DOUBT] A table, I can believe. / A table the size of a stadium?

## BEAT 6: THE WITNESSES (3:20 to 4:00) | Gerald and the poet
Source tag: "Gerald of Wales, Itinerary through Wales, 1188 (Hoare translation); Hallam Tennyson, Memoir, 1897"
L22 [CURATOR] In eleven eighty-eight, / Gerald of Wales visited the ruins at Caerleon. / He listed palaces, baths, temples and theatres. / He never mentions a Round Table.
L23 [DIG] Then, in eighteen fifty-six, / the poet Tennyson stays in Caerleon, and writes in a letter: / "I sit like King Arthur in Caerleon!"
L24 [DOUBT] Lovely sentence. / He does not mention the amphitheatre either.

## BEAT 7: THE NEWSPAPER (4:00 to 4:50) | the dig of 1926
Source tag: "Cadw, Trailblazers: Tessa Verney Wheeler (booklet), pp. 22 to 25; Coflein"
L25 [CURATOR] In nineteen twenty-six, the amphitheatre was dug out / amid a blaze of publicity.
L26 [CLERK] [S] Source: Cadw. / The Daily Mail paid for the dig, / in return for exclusive updates.
L27 [DOUBT] Cadw says the Wheelers / "were canny enough not to dispute this romantic idea."
L28 [CURATOR] [S] Cadw's own booklet calls the Arthur story / "an appealing, though untrue, story."
L29 [DIG] But the digging was real! / Cadw says Tessa Wheeler, Mortimer Wheeler's wife, did the actual excavation!
L30 [DIG] And they found a lead curse tablet!
L31 [CLERK] Cadw's translation: / "Lady Nemesis, I give thee a cloak and a pair of boots. / Let him who took them not redeem them, unless with his own blood."
L32 [DOUBT] Now that is evidence.

## BEAT 8: THE EVIDENCE BOARD (4:50 to 5:15) | pin-board
Source tag: "RCAHMW Historic Place Names (Ordnance Survey 2nd edition)"
L33 [CLERK] Roman amphitheatre: found. / Round Table in Geoffrey: not in the text. / Amphitheatre called the Round Table: / the earliest firm label we found / is on maps from around nineteen hundred.
L34 [CLERK] We could not find when the name began.

## BEAT 9: THE VERDICT | slow push-in, stamp
L35 [CURATOR] The court finds: / the Romans are real. / The arena is real. / The Round Table link is unproven.
L36 [CURATOR] [S] Verdict: / unproven.
L37 [DIG] [S] But it is a great story!
L38 [DOUBT] [S] Great stories sell newspapers.

## BEAT 10: NEXT CASE
L39 [CURATOR] Next time: [PART 3 CASE, TO BE CHOSEN]. / Case adjourned.
