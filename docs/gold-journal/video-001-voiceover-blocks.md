## Block 2: gj-001-ch1

```
Entry two. The five thousand dollar equation.

Price times buyers equals five thousand. That is the whole thing. Pick a price, and the number of people you need appears. Fifty dollars a product needs one hundred buyers. Five hundred dollars needs ten. A thousand dollars needs five.

Same destination, three very different roads. The first is cheap and needs an audience. The second needs trust and a clear result. The third needs a skill and a few good conversations.

Most people never do this sum. They chase "make money online" as if it were a place instead of a number.

Here is the exercise. Write your three versions on a page. Then ask one honest question: which of these could I actually reach, in my life, with the time I have? That is your starting road.

A dream is a destination without arithmetic.

Rule: choose the equation before the business.
```

## Block 3: gj-001-ch2

```
Entry three. Why most people stop.

Not because they are lazy. Usually it is one of four leaks.

Leak one: shiny objects. This week it is dropshipping, next week a newsletter, then a channel. Each switch resets the clock to zero.

Leak two: no distribution. They build something, publish it quietly, and wait. Nobody can buy what nobody has seen.

Leak three: wrong fit. A shy person forcing cold calls. Or a perfectionist who needs to ship ten rough videos and ships none.

Leak four is the sneaky one: quitting in the flat zone. Early work looks like nothing. Zero views, zero sales, for weeks. That is not proof it fails. It is what the start of compounding looks like. People leave one week before the line bends, and never find out where it would have gone.

I have read many journals. Most of the abandoned ones stop at page nine.

Rule: failing slowly is still failing. Switching quickly is how it happens.
```

## Block 4: gj-001-ch3

```
Entry four. Choosing a business that suits you. Not the loudest one. Yours.

Score each path from one to five on four things: skill, time, cash, and temperament. Skill is what you can already do. Time is the hours you truly have. Cash is what you can risk without flinching. Temperament is what you can keep doing when it is boring.

Now the paths. Freelance work sells a skill you already have. It is the fastest road to a first dollar, and it needs little more than a profile and some proof.

Digital products, such as templates or short guides, take longer to build, but can sell while you sleep once people trust you.

Affiliate content means recommending tools you actually use and earning a commission when someone buys through your link. It is slower, because trust takes time, and you must always disclose the link. But it is honest, it scales, and you do not need your own product.

Local services are for people who like talking to people. Print on demand and ecommerce need more cash and more testing.

None of these is the best. The best one is the one your score says you will still be doing in ninety days.

Fashionable is not a fit.

Rule: match the road to the traveller.
```

## Block 5: gj-001-ch4

```
Entry five. How to raise your odds. One business. One channel. Ninety days.

One business, so your effort stacks instead of scattering. One channel, such as a video channel or a single marketplace, so you learn one audience properly. Ninety days, because that is long enough to leave the flat zone and short enough to survive it.

During that time you do not pivot because you are bored. You may change only on data: a small, specific thing the numbers actually say.

Put the ninety days on a calendar, in ink. Block the hours the way you would for a doctor.

A plan you can see gets done. A plan you can only feel gets postponed.

Rule: narrow the road. Lengthen the time.
```

## Block 6: gj-001-ch5

```
Entry six. Consistency is not intensity. It is the smallest action you can repeat when you do not feel like it. One post. Five messages. Thirty minutes of building. Pick a minimum and keep it, even on bad days.

Then add the loop that makes you improve: ship, measure, fix. Ship something. Look at one number. Change one thing.

Every Friday, fifteen minutes, three lines: what moved, what did not, and the one change for next week. Small changes, repeated, beat a brilliant plan that is abandoned.

And a gentle warning. Your first month will probably look unimpressive. That is allowed. You are not behind. You are early.

Compound interest is mostly patience wearing a suit.

Rule: ship, measure, fix. Repeat on Friday.
```
